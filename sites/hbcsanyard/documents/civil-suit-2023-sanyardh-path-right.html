Case status

Suit for declaration and permanent prohibitory injunction (and consequential relief) under Ss. 34, 38 & 39 of the Specific Relief Act; temporary injunction application under Order 39 Rules 1 & 2 CPC.

Section 2

Snapshot — two sides of the dispute

Plaintiffs (village side)

  • Ancestral / long-used path through what became colony land; acquisition did not wipe the path right.
  • 2012 NOC / link connected village road with colony road; used as approach thereafter.
  • Oct 2022–Jan 2023: Plot 12-A owners obstructed / gated the path; seek declaration + removal of obstructions + injunction.
  • SDM 29.11.2022 emergency-only / consent conditions wrongly cut down their rights.

Defendants (HIMUDA / colony / 12-A)

  • Acquisition free from encumbrances; no pre-existing path right through colony land.
  • 2012 NOC was conditional: ambulance only, no permanency, no disturbance — later withdrawn after RWA complaints (28.09.2022).
  • Colony road is maintained from allottees’ charges; village side has another approach via Sanyardh–Kahanwal road.
  • Gate / fencing said to protect colony; SDM order allowed a gate; matter later said to favour respondents.

Section 5

Plaint (complaint) — plaintiffs’ case

Suit land (as pleaded)

Muhal Sanyardh, Hadbast No. 363, Patwar Circle Manyana, Tehsil Sadar, District Mandi.

  • 1(a) — older holdings / joint ownership context of plaintiff no. 1 & co-owners.
  • 1(b) — land later recorded with HIMUDA after acquisition for Himuda Housing Colony Sanyard (mutation 676 dated 9.9.2003).
  • 1(c) — remaining land of plaintiffs / co-owners needing access (includes Khasra 676/68 among other parcels).

Core plea: acquisition of 1(b) for the colony did not extinguish existing path rights.

Storyline in the plaint

  • Long open / continuous / peaceful use of the path as of right (pleaded 60+ years / ancestral).
  • After acquisition, path on colony land developed into a road; village use continued.
  • 5.3.2012 NOC / permission to link existing path with colony road; State construction of village approach connected at Khasra 676/68; peaceful use since 2012 pleaded as the practical approach.
  • Oct 2022: defendants 4 & 5 allegedly began damaging / obstructing the path.
  • 29.11.2022: SDM Sadar conditional order (emergency use; gate allowed; use with defendants’ consent) — challenged as illegal.
  • 2.1.2023: alleged complete blockade (wall / barbed wire / gate).
  • 10.1.2023: plaint and temporary injunction application filed.

Section 6

Reply — colony / Plot 12-A (defendants 3–5)

Reply on behalf of respondents 3 to 5 (colony side and Plot 12-A owners), with affidavit of Bishan Chand Sharma (Plot 12-A) sworn at Mandi (Dec 2024 papers).

Main defence points

  • No path right through land in plaint para 1(b); allegations of ancestral path denied as bald / false.
  • Land acquired under the Land Acquisition Act is free from encumbrances (ss. 16–17 cited); any encumbrance ends on acquisition (Supreme Court grazing / estate-right analogy pleaded).
  • Colony internal roads built for allottees; village Jol road allegedly had no connection until a conditional 2012 NOC.
  • NOC text (as quoted): link existing path with HIMUDA colony road only if — (1) panchayat will not claim permanency; (2) use only for ambulance; (3) no disturbance to colony residents.
  • After NOC, villagers allegedly used the road for ordinary access, parked vehicles, caused night nuisance — violating conditions.
  • RWA / residents pay annual maintenance (thousands of rupees); HIMUDA later told association permission was temporary ambulance-only and withdrew it — so even ambulance use no longer authorised on their case.
  • Site plan of colony does not depict the claimed path; alternative village approach via Sanyardh–Kahanwal road exists; plaintiffs seek a short-cut through private / colony land.
  • Plot 12-A owners deny creating illegal obstruction; say plaintiffs were trespassing; SDM emergency order allowed a gate; Sessions Court remanded; SDM later held in favour of respondents — no current SDM order in plaintiffs’ favour (as pleaded in this reply).
  • Temporary injunction: deny prima facie case, balance of convenience, irreparable injury; pray dismissal of injunction application.

Section 7

Reply — HIMUDA

HIMUDA reply to the suit / Order 39 application (Civil Suit papers marked ≈ 086/2023), affidavit of Anjori Kapoor, Chief Engineer, HIMUDA (Dec 2024).

Main defence points

  • On acquisition / purchase, land vendors’ rights (including access through HIMUDA land) were settled with compensation / mutual consent.
  • NOC dated 05.03.2012 (letter No. HIMUDA/MD/CB/W-42/12-9623-25) allowed Gram Panchayat Sanyard to link path for ambulance only, with no permanency and no disturbance — to protect allottees who paid for plots / flats under tenancy agreements.
  • NOC withdrawn after RWA complaints dated 28.09.2022, relying on the “no disturbance” condition.
  • SDM order 29.11.2022: emergency use for villagers; defendants permitted to construct a gate on the boundary of suit land — HIMUDA says it appreciated the order as a law-abiding statutory authority (H.P. Housing & Urban Development Authority Act, 2004).
  • Gate on the disputed road constructed by the Residents Welfare Association; HIMUDA assisted; colony maintenance handed over to Municipal Corporation, Mandi on 23.04.2022.
  • Prays for appropriate orders / costs in favour of HIMUDA respondents.

Section 8

Replication — plaintiff (to WS of D3–D5)

Replication dated 2.12.2024 (Place: Mandi), through counsel D.C. Guleria & Harish Guleria, answering the written statement of defendants 3 to 5.

What the plaintiff reaffirms / denies

  • Preliminary objections (no cause of action, unclean hands, estoppel, vague allegations) — all denied; suit maintainable.
  • Path through 1(b)/1(c) reaffirmed; denial of path right rejected.
  • Admits there was a path later converted to road: Gram Panchayat portion outside acquired land; HIMUDA portion inside colony; connected in 2012 on XEN NOC.
  • Defendants had no legal right to block with a gate, or to withdraw the NOC without hearing affected parties.
  • Purchasers of Plot 12-A had no right to block the path arbitrarily; SDM order does not wipe the plaintiff’s path right.
  • Denies nuisance / disturbance allegations; denies existence of adequate alternative approach; denies that no path existed when 12-A was purchased.
  • Denies right to fix barbed wire / boundary wall to stop path use; reaffirms cause of action, valuation, and prayer to decree the suit.

Section 9

What the court still has to decide (live issues)

  1. Was there a pre-acquisition right of path through the land that became the colony, and did acquisition extinguish it?
  2. What legal effect does the 5.3.2012 NOC have — ambulance-only licence, or recognition / continuation of a broader access practice?
  3. Was withdrawal of the NOC (after 28.09.2022 RWA complaint) lawful, and were affected villagers entitled to a hearing?
  4. Are gate / barbed wire / boundary works near Plot 12-A / Khasra 676/68 lawful protections or illegal obstruction?
  5. What is the binding effect of the SDM 29.11.2022 order (and any later SDM / Sessions steps pleaded by the parties)?
  6. Do plaintiffs have an adequate alternative approach (Sanyardh–Kahanwal side), or is the colony link the necessary approach as pleaded?

Until final judgment / decree, treat access arrangements as litigation-sensitive and follow current court / authority directions on the ground.
